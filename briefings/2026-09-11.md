## OpenAI's Astra crosses "Critical" cyber threshold; Google DeepMind ships a defenders-only Gemini variant

OpenAI classified its upcoming Astra model at "Critical" under its Preparedness Framework — the company says it can independently identify and exploit previously unknown vulnerabilities across hardened systems with limited human involvement, and OpenAI delayed parts of the release to build and test safeguards, restricting access to the model's advanced cyber capabilities. Google DeepMind followed days later with a defenders-only "Cyber" variant of Gemini 3.8 Flash, while stating its public models remain below its own Critical Capability Level thresholds.

**Source:** MarkTechPost — https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/09/03/openai-releases-gpt-6-astra-a-1-05m-context-computer-use-model-gated-behind-a-critical-cyber-threshold/ ; The Hacker News — https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/google-anthropic-and-openai-unveil.html

**Why this matters:** This is the first time a major lab's own safety framework has triggered real access restrictions on cyber-offensive capability, not just a benchmark disclosure. Clients relying on frontier model APIs should add "has this vendor crossed a capability threshold" to their vendor-risk monitoring — it's a leading indicator of what access tiers, KYC requirements, or usage restrictions may show up in your contract next, and of what capability is now known to exist even if gated.

## Agent identity / kill-switch tooling reaches production maturity

Two separate vendors shipped real (not just announced) runtime controls for AI agents this week: Akeyless reached general availability on "Agentic Runtime Authority," adding intent-based access control and a live session-termination dashboard on top of its credential-protection layer, with integrations for Claude Enterprise, OpenAI Codex, and Amazon Bedrock AgentCore. Separately, Orchid Security added identity drift detection and application-level kill switches to its Identity Control Plane, letting security teams strip an agent's authority the moment its behavior deviates from what was approved.

**Source:** Help Net Security — https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/09/akeyless-agentic-runtime-authority-identity-control-layer/ ; https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/09/orchid-security-ai-agents-application-level-kill-switches/

**Why this matters:** This is a genuine maturity signal, not just launch noise — both ship with production integrations and an actual kill-switch/session-termination mechanism, which is the specific capability most NHI governance advice has been recommending in the abstract. Worth a bake-off if a client hasn't already picked an agent-identity vendor.

## House Intelligence Committee warns AI could be the next "failure of imagination"; FBI says China is already inside the grid

A bipartisan House Intelligence Committee report, released around the 9/11 anniversary, warns AI could become the same kind of national-security blind spot that preceded the 2001 attacks, citing testimony that AI is moving faster than government can adapt and that the most relevant data now sits with private companies. Separately, FBI Director Kash Patel testified that Chinese campaigns (Salt Typhoon, Volt Typhoon) have already penetrated US water, energy, grid, and telecom infrastructure — a general cyber/critical-infrastructure finding, not an AI-specific compromise, but cited in the same report as part of the "failure of imagination" argument.

**Source:** NewsNation — https://www.newsnationnow.com/business/tech/national-security-house-intelligence-committee-ai-china-hacking/

**Why this matters:** This is signal about how seriously Washington is now treating AI risk at the political level (relevant to where regulatory pressure heads next), but it's not a concrete technical or architectural change — treat it as context for client conversations about the political mood, not as a control you need to implement.
