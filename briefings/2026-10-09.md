## 1. ARTEX AI-driven breach of seven South Korean banks — a scope judgment call for Section 7, not a Section 3 candidate

CrowdStrike published an analysis Oct 7, 2026 (broader coverage via The Hacker News and Korean outlets Oct 7–8) identifying ARTEX AI — an open-source, LLM-based autonomous penetration-testing tool distributed on GitHub, aimed at Chinese-speaking users and able to connect to DeepSeek, OpenAI, and Anthropic models — as the tool behind breaches at seven South Korean financial institutions (including Shinhan Bank, KB Kookmin, Hana Bank) between late September and early October 2026. Shinhan alone reported roughly 25,000 affected customers (names, phone numbers, income, loan limits); total affected across all seven institutions is reported as 66,000–68,000 people depending on source. CrowdStrike's analysis of exposed open directories reportedly found Claude Code session histories, ARTEX configuration files, and Claude memory files — meaning the attacker's own agentic tooling, built on commercial frontier models, left forensic traces of its own operation. CrowdStrike assesses with moderate confidence that the threat actor is a financially motivated Chinese speaker; attribution and whether ARTEX acted autonomously versus under heavy human direction both remain unconfirmed. AhnLab separately found ARTEX instances across roughly 600 IP addresses worldwide, suggesting broader deployment beyond this campaign.

- Source: [CrowdStrike](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/unknown-threat-actor-uses-artex-to-target-south-korean-finance/), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/artex-ai-pentesting-tool-used-in-data.html), [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/banking-finance/20261007/explainer-how-ai-emerged-as-new-threat-in-koreas-bank-hacking-crisis)
- **Why this matters:** Every existing Section 3 row models a failure in an enterprise's own deployed-agent architecture. This is the inverse — a criminal actor's own offensive tool, built on the same commercial model infrastructure enterprises use defensively. The document's one existing precedent for this attacker-wielded-AI pattern is the "Dual-Use Model Misuse — Threat-Actor Weapons Development" subsection in Section 7, but that's scoped to weapons/CBRN misuse specifically, not financial-sector cybercrime. Three options are laid out in the pending doc: broaden that subsection's scope, add a narrower new subsection for AI-driven cybercrime, or exclude entirely as one step too far from the document's enterprise-agent-architecture focus.

*Noted and excluded:* "Autonomous AI Agents Compromise Thousands of Credentials in Under Six Hours" (The Hacker News) resurfaced in a weekly-recap aggregator piece this week, but the underlying disclosure is from September and falls into the same attacker-wielded-AI category as ARTEX, without the same scale or primary-source depth — not independently flagged.
