## 1. GitBook infrastructure breach, described by the company as a targeted attack using AI agents (disclosed Nov 2, 2026)

GitBook — a documentation platform widely used by crypto/developer projects (PancakeSwap among its customers) — detected a security incident affecting its infrastructure around 19:00 CET on Nov 2. GitBook says it contained the intrusion quickly and took some services offline as a precaution; app, API, and published docs were restored by Nov 3. Potentially exposed: integration tokens, Git Sync secrets, API tokens, and questions submitted to GitBook's own AI assistant. GitBook found no evidence customer content was altered, and asked all customers to rotate API tokens/integration secrets and re-authenticate any connected MCP server. PancakeSwap moved its documentation off GitBook in response.

**Sources:** [GitBook's own status notice](https://gitbook.com/status/security-incident-2026-11-03) | [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/382465/crypto-docs-gitbook-ai-agent-security-breach) | [CoinEdition](https://coinedition.com/crypto-live-news/gitbook-ai-attack-exposes-api-secrets-triggers-urgent/)

**Why this matters:** fits the Section 7 Dual-Use Misuse pattern (ARTEX/Cairn/CrowdStrike) — a company's own account naming AI agents as the attack method, which is more specific framing than Chainalysis's vaguer "offensive agentic techniques" language, though GitBook hasn't yet published its promised post-mortem or named the specific tooling/root cause. Proposing as Edit 2 in the pending doc, with that sourcing caveat flagged explicitly. Also a modest coverage gap — missed by the Nov 3, 4, and 5 runs, all of which actively searched and didn't surface it.
