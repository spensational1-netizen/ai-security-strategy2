## Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google hold talks on a joint AI safety standards body

The Washington Post and The Information reported (Sept 13-14) that Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google have held working-group meetings since July exploring an industry-led standards body for frontier AI — reportedly centered on shared protocols for pre-release testing: independent evaluations, pre-release safety reviews, and standardized risk assessments. The effort is said to be led by Anthropic's Dario Amodei, with Sam Altman supportive of an industry-led approach "especially in the absence of government support." Per reporting, nothing has been finalized — no charter, no rulebook, no launch date. The same week, Amodei separately warned that within 6-12 months AI could be capable of directing a swarm of agents at internet scale, and China's top intelligence official issued unusually direct public warnings about AI risk to political and infrastructure stability.

**Source:** The Washington Post — https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/09/14/anthropic-openai-google-discussed-creating-new-ai-safety-body/ ; Bloomberg — https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/china-spy-chief-warns-of-ai-risks-as-us-tech-leaders-urge-brakes

**Why this matters:** This is upstream, vendor-side governance — a self-regulatory body for how labs test models before release, not a control an enterprise deploys. It's worth monitoring because a ratified industry standard could eventually become a reference point in vendor contracts or procurement questionnaires ("does your model provider participate in X pre-release testing regime?"), but there's nothing here yet to act on. Treat today's coverage as three rival labs signaling intent under public and political pressure, not as an emerging compliance requirement.
