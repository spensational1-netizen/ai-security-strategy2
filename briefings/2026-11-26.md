## 1. CERT-In: "Crafty Falcon" AI-assisted threat actor targeting Indian NBFCs, fintechs, payment aggregators, and crypto exchanges (CIAD-2026-0058, Nov 4, 2026; updates CIAD-2026-0046, Oct 9, 2026)

India's national CERT, via its financial-sector unit CSIRT-Fin, issued a Critical-rated advisory on Nov 4, 2026 naming "Crafty Falcon" as an AI-assisted financial threat actor, updating an Oct 9, 2026 original advisory on payment-API abuse. Crafty Falcon typically gains initial access through internet-exposed non-production environments (UAT/staging/testing), then uses a range of AI and agentic-AI penetration-testing tools — some tooling reportedly linked to Chinese origin, though attribution of the operator itself is not established — to reconnoiter targets and invoke the victims' own payment/disbursement APIs, testing with micro-value transactions (e.g., ₹1) before larger fraudulent transfers. For crypto exchanges and VDA providers, the group is reported to steal wallet keys and drain wallets. Attacks cluster during holidays, weekends, and other low-monitoring windows. CERT-In says agentic tooling has shortened the time from initial access to final impact from days to hours, and that the campaign remains active — institutions are reporting successful attacks even after taking corrective measures CERT-In had already recommended.

**Source:** CERT-In advisory CIAD-2026-0058 (Nov 4, 2026), corroborated by consistent independent secondary coverage (Infosecurity Magazine, Moneylife, Inc42, YourStory, Cyble, GKToday). **Sourcing caveat:** this session could not directly retrieve CERT-In's primary advisory text (cert-in.org.in is outside this environment's reachable domains, the same restriction hit on aws.amazon.com yesterday and OWASP/arxiv previously), so these facts rest on WebSearch's synthesis of multiple independent, mutually consistent secondary accounts of the same government advisory — not a direct read of CERT-In's own text.

**Why this matters:** a third documented case — after the Aur0ra/Cursor case (already in Revision 4.2) and yesterday's proposed Amazon ARTEX/LiteLLM/Dify edit — of named, real-world criminal operators running agentic AI tools offensively against third-party (here, financial-sector) infrastructure, this one confirmed by a national CERT rather than a vendor's own threat-intel team. Unlike the retracted Railway item, the attack vector itself is AI-specific (autonomous reconnaissance and exploitation-acceleration against payment APIs), not merely an AI-related motive layered onto a conventional breach — so it clears this document's "names a specific attack vector" bar outright. Proposed for Section 7, Dual-Use Misuse subsection.
