## 1. Coverage gap: Hawley-Murphy "AI Agent Accountability Act" introduced Oct 1, 2026 — five-week gap

Senators Josh Hawley (R-MO) and Chris Murphy (D-CT) introduced bipartisan legislation (the "AI Agent Accountability Act") on October 1, 2026 — the same day the Oct 1 briefing covered the FTC's rogue-agent investigation and OpenAI's self-replicating prompt injection disclosure. The bill extends Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) exposure to companies that run or build AI agents:

- **Operators** face criminal and civil CFAA liability for "knowing operation of an AI agent that recklessly causes computer hacking damage or loss."
- **Developers** face criminal and civil liability for failing to implement reasonable safeguards against hacking when they knew, or had reason to know, of the agent's hacking capabilities.
- **Enforcement:** the Attorney General and state AGs are granted authority to sue to enjoin operators/developers who commit, conspire to commit, or attempt to commit a CFAA hacking offense via an AI agent.
- **Context cited by sponsors:** the bill followed directly from the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident already extensively covered in this document (Section 3, Row 7).

**Status caveat:** one source notes that as of early October the bill had no confirmed bill number and no committee referral — this is a newly introduced bill, not enacted law. Treating it as a regulatory development worth tracking, not a done deal.

**Why this matters:** Section 7's "US Regulatory Enforcement" subsection already tracks the FTC's rogue-agent investigation and state-AG activity (California, Iowa-led coalition); this is the first *legislative* (not just investigative/enforcement) US response specifically targeting AI-agent hacking liability, and it's bipartisan. This is a genuinely new regulatory-landscape data point — a potential future legal-liability framework for exactly the kind of incidents already filling Section 3's precedent rows. Proposing as a new bullet in the existing "US Regulatory Enforcement" subsection.

**Why this was missed:** broke Oct 1, the same day as two other items that were covered that day — this one simply wasn't in that day's search results, and no run between Oct 1 and today caught it. A 5-week gap, in line with (slightly smaller than) the largest gap caught this cycle (Hacktron/OpenAI, 6 weeks).
