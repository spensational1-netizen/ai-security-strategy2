## 1. Microsoft Research/Red Team: "Lazarus" — backdooring an AI agent by editing fewer than 10 neurons, no retraining required (published Nov 10, 2026)

Microsoft Research and Microsoft's Red Team published a technique, informally dubbed "Lazarus," for implanting a hidden backdoor in an AI model by directly editing fewer than 10 of its rarely-used ("dormant") neurons — no retraining, no fine-tuning dataset. Tested on three open-weight models, including Qwen variants and Nemotron-Terminal-8B (sources differ slightly on exact neuron counts per model, converging on "fewer than 10"). The backdoored models pass standard evaluations largely unchanged (one Qwen variant kept most of its AIME math score; Terminal-Bench pass rate dipped from 46.59% to 42.05%) — meaning the tampering is not reliably caught by benchmark-based model vetting. One demonstrated trigger is time-based: once the system clock passes into 2027, the agent emits a command to download and execute attacker-supplied code. Microsoft credits Claude Code (running Opus 4.5) with helping find the core idea during the research process. Microsoft states it has not observed this technique weaponized by real threat actors. Recommended mitigations: compare any deployed checkpoint against its original unmodified weights (not just benchmark scores), keep approved models in a private registry rather than pulling directly from public hubs, and sandbox agent-generated code execution.

**Source:** Microsoft's own research publication (Nov 10, 2026), corroborated extensively (The Hacker News, TheNextWeb, InfoWorld, CSO Online, Help Net Security, AIWeekly, LetsDataScience, CryptoSlate, SecNews.gr) — strong, consistent primary-adjacent sourcing.
