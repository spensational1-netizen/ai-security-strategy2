## 1. Correction: GitBook breach root cause is SSO session forgery, not AI agents — retracting the Nov 6 proposed edit

When I proposed the GitBook bullet on Nov 6, the only available sourcing was GitBook's initial status notice and early crypto-press coverage (Decrypt, CoinEdition) characterizing the Nov 2 breach as "a targeted attack using AI agents," with GitBook itself not yet having published technical detail. GitBook has since published a fuller post-incident review (reported by Odaily, RootData, and a Blocktempo/Penligent write-up, postmortem dated ~Nov 4). That review describes a concrete, conventional attack chain: an attacker exploited a flaw in GitBook's SSO login flow to forge an employee session, used that session to reach a misconfigured GitHub App credential, and pivoted to a Cloudflare API key and production secrets. None of this newer, more detailed coverage repeats the "AI agents" framing at all — it's a standard SSO/credential-chain attack with nothing specific to AI agents.

**Sources:** [Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/newsflash/531109) | [RootData](https://www.rootdata.com/news/817040) | [GitBook's status page](https://gitbook.com/status/security-incident-2026-11-03)

**Why this matters:** I haven't located GitBook's primary post-mortem text directly (same sourcing limitation the original item had), but three independent secondary sources now converge on the SSO explanation with specific technical detail, and nothing published since Nov 4 repeats the AI-agent claim. That's strong enough to retract rather than leave a contradicted item in the pending queue. Removed it from the pending doc's proposed edits and documented the retraction there, following the same self-correction discipline used for the "Lightning AI" item on Nov 2.
